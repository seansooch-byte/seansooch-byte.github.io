i

Prologue

Purpose, scope, and method

Purpose

This record documents five months of learning and building in spare hours. These projects were made possible by tools that lowered the technical barrier between an idea and working software. Others will bring different experience and build better things.

First deploy April 2026. Everything since, in evenings and weekends.

Scope

Everything listed is deployed and public. The page does not take that on faith: when it loads, your browser asks every host listed under Attestation to answer, and records what came back. The Works follow by domain, then the Writings, the lesser works, and the films. The Editions are the change record for the site itself.

A green mark means a host answered, not that its page is right. See the caution.

Method

Every system was specified by the author and written with Claude, then verified the way a clinician verifies a lab: against a source, by a second reader, and against the live deployment rather than the local copy. Medical content carries its citations. Scoring tables cite the governing publication. Where a source deck was wrong, the question was corrected and the correction logged.

NBStudy tools here are for exam preparation and are not medical advice. Calculators and field references are teaching aids; confirm with the governing guideline before use on a patient.

The Frontier AI Risk Stack

Frontier AI risk is best understood not as a single category but as a stack of six interacting layers. The most serious dangers arise from the way failures compound across layers: a structured analytical tool for policymakers, developers, and civil society.

Read on the AGI TrackerTechnical PDFPlain-language PDF

  1. Model riskdeception, unsafe knowledge, concealment
  2. System riskorchestration, tool use, parallel execution
  3. Deployment riskreal permissions, production access
  4. Access riskconcentration, cognitive inequality
  5. Governance riskweak audits, regulatory capture
  6. Civilizational riskcompounding failures across all layers

Three Roads From Here

The three positions on frontier AI (safety through development, acceleration, pause) characterized on their strongest terms, with existential and suffering risk treated as separate axes that do not always move together. The central frame: the camps are reading three different payoff matrices for the same game.

Two Stories About the Beginning

The Big Bang and a generated origin are the same kind of story: a starting condition plus a rule, with the evidence only ever reaching the rule. Four objections (tidiness, cheating, motive, proving too much) are taken seriously; two of them force the second story to change, and it survives as a testable rival rather than an evasion.

  4. Motivedropped; the process only has to exist and run

Editions

The change record, drawn as a family tree

One redesign per model class. Retired editions stay online.
The practice

This site is rebuilt from the ground up at every new Anthropic model class, as a personal benchmark of frontier capability. Each edition is designed end to end by the model it is named for, then retired, not deleted, when its successor arrives. Put two side by side and you can see, in typography and judgment rather than benchmark scores, how far the frontier moved. Vol. 03 changed the practice: when the next class arrived it was not retired. The Field Manual stays current beside OpenAI Astra's Personal Index; this Commentary joined them third, and Cursor Composer 2.5's Live Stack and Grok's Night Orbit after it, so Vol. 03 has five views drawn by five hands, and you can switch between them without losing your place.

A stemma is the family tree an editor draws of the surviving copies of a text. Each copy is a witness, named by a letter, its siglum.
