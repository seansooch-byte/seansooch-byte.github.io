The Lab

Core medicine, one picture at a time. Each lesson takes a single idea, starting with how the heart fills and empties, builds it step by step in plain language with moving pictures, then asks you to predict what each disease and drug class will do before it shows you.

Every lesson opens with what you will be able to do and how long it takes, ends with a quiz and a one-page summary, and has a Clinical values tab: typical adult reference ranges from standard textbooks and guidelines, each one cited, drawn against the lesson as it runs. Educational only and not for clinical use; no drug doses. Your progress stays in this browser: no accounts, no trackers, and it works on a phone and with a keyboard.

Unit 1 · 5 lessons · about 2 hours

The heart, the kidney and blood pressure

How the heart fills and pumps, how the kidney handles salt and water, and the reflexes and hormones that hold blood pressure steady.

  1. 01

    The Pressure–Volume Loop

    How one heartbeat fills and empties the left ventricle, drawn as a loop, and how fluids, blood pressure and heart drugs reshape it.

    30 min · 18 steps · 8 questions · A good place to start

    What you will be able to do
    1. Trace one heartbeat round the pressure-volume loop: name its four phases and say what each valve is doing.
    2. Read stroke volume and ejection fraction off a loop.
    3. Explain which part of the loop preload, afterload and contractility each control.
    4. Predict how fluids, a vasoconstrictor, a positive inotrope, a venodilator, a beta-blocker and heart failure with reduced ejection fraction change the loop.

    Not started

  2. 02

    The Nephron and Diuretics

    Follow salt and water along a nephron, segment by segment, and see where each diuretic blocks salt reabsorption and how much water follows it out.

    25 min · 17 steps · 8 questions · A good place to start

    What you will be able to do
    1. Trace salt and water along a nephron and name what each segment takes back.
    2. Explain why water follows salt, and how two hormones set what the collecting duct keeps.
    3. Predict how much each diuretic class adds to the salt and water in the urine, and what that does to blood volume.
    4. Tell the diuretic classes apart by what they do to blood potassium and bicarbonate.

    Not started

  3. 03

    Frank–Starling and Cardiac Output

    Why a fuller heart pumps harder, and how filling, heart rate and contraction strength add up to cardiac output.

    25 min · 16 steps · 8 questions · Builds on Lesson 1

    What you will be able to do
    1. Explain how filling stretches heart muscle so the next beat pumps more: the Frank–Starling mechanism.
    2. Read the Frank–Starling curve, telling a move along it (a change in filling) apart from a shift of the whole curve (a change in contractility).
    3. Explain how stroke volume and heart rate make cardiac output, and why a very fast heart can pump less.
    4. Predict how IV fluids, diuretics, positive inotropes, beta-blockers, venodilators and heart failure change blood per beat and cardiac output.

    Not started

  4. 04

    Blood Pressure Regulation

    Blood pressure as flow times resistance, and how the baroreflex keeps it steady from one moment to the next.

    26 min · 17 steps · 8 questions · Builds on Lessons 1 and 3

    What you will be able to do
    1. Explain blood pressure as flow times resistance, and name what sets each: heart rate, blood per beat and blood volume for flow; arteriole width for resistance.
    2. Read a pressure trace: the systolic peak, the diastolic low point and the mean.
    3. Trace the baroreflex from the stretch sensors through the brainstem and its nerves, and explain how it steadies pressure on standing up.
    4. Predict, for each blood pressure drug and for blood loss, whether flow or resistance moves, and how the baroreflex answers.

    Not started

  5. 05

    The Renin–Angiotensin–Aldosterone System

    The hormone chain the kidney uses to raise blood pressure and hold on to salt, and the drugs that cut it at each link.

    25 min · 15 steps · 8 questions · Builds on Lessons 2 and 4

    What you will be able to do
    1. Trace the chain from renin to aldosterone: what each link makes, and where.
    2. Explain the two ways angiotensin II raises blood pressure, and how feedback switches the chain off.
    3. Predict how a drug changes the links after its cut, the links before it, and blood potassium.
    4. Tell ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, aldosterone antagonists and direct renin inhibitors apart by where they cut the chain.

    Not started

Nerves, rhythm and muscle

    30 min · 22 steps · 8 questions · A good place to start

    What you will be able to do
    1. Trace one autonomic signal across a synapse: release, crossing the gap, docking on a receptor, and how the gap is cleared.

    4. Predict what agonists, antagonists and cholinesterase inhibitors do to heart rate, blood pressure, airways and pupils, including the baroreflex's answer.

    3. Read the PR interval, the QRS complex and the QT interval as the AV node's pause, the ventricles' phase 0 and their whole action potential.
    4. Predict how class I, II, III and IV drugs reshape the action potential and the electrocardiogram.
