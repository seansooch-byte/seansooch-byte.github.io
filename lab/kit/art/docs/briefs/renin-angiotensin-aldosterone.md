# Redraw brief: renin-angiotensin-aldosterone (Lesson 5)

Files: `lab/renin-angiotensin-aldosterone/app.js` (518 lines, every scene), `index.html` (15 steps, captions, `<dfn data-part>`), `style.css` (one rule: `.on-organ` text halo `#f6dcd8`).
Shots: `scratchpad/audit/shots/renin-angiotensin-aldosterone/sNN-<kind>-t{0.35,1}.png`.

Overall grade: **3/10**. Steps 1 to 9 are the anatomy/cell pictures and read as clip art: a bean outline for the kidney, a triangle for the adrenal, a pink rectangle for a lung capillary, a U-shaped cup for a receptor, a cream rounded box for a principal cell, a beaker for blood volume, Pac-Man discs for enzymes. Steps 10 to 15 share one bar-chart relay (`chainMap`) that is honest and model-driven and is the strongest picture in the lesson (6/10), but has collisions and a wrong legend. The single biggest gap: none of the organs or cells is recognisable, and the molecular players (receptor, channels, steroid) have no real shape.

## Global rules for the redraw

- Keep every scene's signature (`{duration, loop, render(t)}`), every `svg(label, body, {h})` height (all <= 420) and every `aria-label`, updated to describe the new picture.
- Keep these `data-part` names, used by `<dfn>` in index.html: `kidney, pressure, sensor, renin, angiotensinogen, ang1, ace, ang2, receptor, arteriole, resistance, adrenal, aldosterone, nephron, sodium, potassium, blood-volume, feedback, block`. Each must wrap the visible structure, not just a label.
- Keep the ghost convention `<g class="ghost" data-ghost="normal">` (dial needle, bars, arteriole wall, volume level) and `data-block`, `data-named`, `data-key` group attributes in `chainMap` (kit-wide convention; no tool asserts them, but other lessons use them).
- Molecule vocabulary (keep, but upgrade): angiotensin I = 10 pale-blue beads, angiotensin II = 8 solid-blue beads (counts are correct: decapeptide -> octapeptide; ACE removes 2). Renin and ACE green. Aldosterone green. Na+ amber diamond, K+ purple circle (matches Lesson 2). Do not change these colour meanings.
- New shared tissue palette (add to `draw.js` or a shared `anatomy.js`): kidney cortex `#c9645a` -> medulla pyramids `#a8453f` with a radial gradient highlight; capsule stroke `#7a2a24`; adrenal cortex `#e7b94a` with medulla `#b07a2a`; arterial blood `#b3261e` with lumen highlight `#d9483f`; venous blood `#4a4f7a` (not the current flat grey `#5d6270`); smooth muscle `#c98a6a` with fibre striations; cytoplasm `#fbefe2`; nucleus `#d9c7e8` with `#6b3fa0` rim; membrane heads `#e7a9a0`, tails `#f4d9c9`. Shading: one linear or radial gradient per organ (light upper-left), 1.5 px inner highlight stroke at 40 % white, line weights 2.5 organ outline / 1.5 internal / 1 hairline leaders.
- Leader lines: 1 px ink, ending in a 2.5 r dot on the structure, label set at a clear margin; no label may sit on an arrow or another label.
- Type: `.lg 24 / default 20 / .sm 18 / .xs 17`, never smaller.

## Step-by-step

### Step 1 `watch` (grade 3)
Problems: kidney is a flat pink bean with no cortex, medulla, pelvis or ureter; "sensor cells" are four green dots floating at the hilum outside the organ, but juxtaglomerular cells sit in the wall of the afferent arteriole deep in the cortex at the glomerulus, so the picture places them wrongly; artery and vein are flat bars (vein a dull grey); "blood in" label sits on top of the dial post; dial post is pinned to the artery like a lamp; nothing animates except a 3 % needle twitch.
Redraw: a two-panel figure. Left (x 10 to 250): a coronal-section kidney (hilum facing right) with a gradient cortex rim, 6 to 7 darker medullary pyramids, renal pelvis funnelling into a ureter going down, renal artery (red, wall + lumen highlight) and renal vein (blue-grey) entering at the hilum. A small square on the cortex marks the zoom region, with two thin zoom lines out to the right panel. Right (x 250 to 470): the zoom, a glomerulus (tuft of capillary loops inside Bowman's capsule) with its afferent arteriole coming in; a cluster of plump granular (juxtaglomerular) cells in the afferent arteriole wall, drawn as cuboidal cells with green granules (`data-part="sensor"`), and a short distal-tubule segment touching it (the macula densa can be drawn but needs no label here, since the caption mentions tube cells only in step 2). Pressure: keep the dial (`data-part="pressure"`) small, at the top of the zoom, reading the afferent arteriole; label "blood pressure" above. Motion: pulse travelling along the afferent arteriole (wall widens slightly per beat) and the needle twitching in sync. Labels: "kidney" (lg), "renal artery", "renal vein", "sensor cells in the arteriole wall" with leader. h about 380.
Keep: `kidneyCloseUp()` is shared with step 2; keep its options (P, ghost, renin, reninLevel, reninGhost).

### Step 2 `renin` (grade 3)
Problems: the renin "stream" is green Pac-Men overlapping the white chevrons in the vein; the renin bar is blue while renin is green everywhere else; the bar floats in empty space; "falls" is a free word with no arrow.
Redraw: same zoom as step 1. Needle falls below its dashed ghost (keep) with a short blue curved arrow and "falls". Granular cells swell; green renin granules exocytose from them into the arteriole lumen and then ride into the renal vein in the left panel (the renin enters the circulation). Bar: make it green fill (renin colour) with the dashed normal outline, labelled "renin released", placed under the zoom panel; add a blue "more" arrow beside it at t=1. Renin glyph: small green enzyme (see primitives) not a Pac-Man.

### Step 3 `cut` (grade 3)
Problems: liver is an oval blob; angiotensinogen is 21 beads with the future angiotensin I already pre-coloured pale blue, which gives the answer away before the cut; there is no blood vessel, though the cut happens in plasma; renin is a Pac-Man below the chain; large dead space bottom-left.
Redraw: top-left a recognisable liver silhouette (right lobe larger, falciform notch, gradient brown-red `#9c4a3a`), with a hepatic vein carrying out into a horizontal plasma channel that spans the frame (pale red wash, vessel walls top and bottom). Angiotensinogen: a long globular protein, drawn as a coiled ribbon of about 30 white beads folding back on itself (to read "large protein"), with its 10-bead N-terminal tail hanging free and drawn in the SAME white until cut. Renin (green enzyme with an active-site cleft) slides along the channel, clamps the tail at the 10/11 junction, a small flash, and the cut tail turns pale blue and drifts right as angiotensin I. Labels: "liver", "angiotensinogen, a large protein from the liver", "renin", "angiotensin I: 10 units, does little by itself". h about 340.

### Step 4 `ace-step` (grade 3)
Problems: "a blood vessel in the lungs" is a pink rectangle with two red lines; ACE are three Pac-Men on the top lining only; the chain passes through the middle of the lumen and never touches ACE, so the trimmed beads appear from nowhere; huge empty bands.
Redraw: top strip shows 2 to 3 alveoli (thin-walled air sacs, pale blue-white) wrapping a pulmonary capillary, so "lungs" is visible. Main field: the capillary in longitudinal section, both walls made of flattened endothelial cells (each with an oval nucleus), ACE drawn as membrane-anchored enzymes sticking into the lumen from the endothelial surface on BOTH walls. Each angiotensin I chain drifts toward the wall, touches an ACE, and the last two beads clip off (they fade as a dipeptide), the remaining 8 recolour to solid blue and continue right. Keep the loop and the two staggered chains. Labels: "lung capillary" (top), "ACE on the lining" with leader to one ACE, "angiotensin I (10)" left, "angiotensin II (8), the active hormone" right, "2 units trimmed" by the falling pair.

### Step 5 `receptor` (grade 3)
Problems: receptor is a thick black U, which teaches the wrong shape (the angiotensin II receptor, AT1, is a seven-transmembrane G-protein-coupled receptor); the membrane is two red lines with circles and no tails; "docks" text collides with the receptor wall; the "answer" is three generic arrows; the right panel repeats the membrane with nothing new.
Redraw: two cells side by side, each a proper lipid bilayer (two leaflets of round heads with paired wavy tails, warm tones) over cytoplasm (with a hint of nucleus at the bottom). Left: a seven-helix receptor drawn as seven rounded vertical cylinders through the bilayer in a soft purple-grey, with an extracellular pocket at the top; the angiotensin II chain (8 blue beads, folded) settles into the pocket. On binding, a G protein (three small subunit blobs) on the inner face lights up and a blue "signal" ripple spreads into the cytoplasm with label "the cell answers". Right: identical bilayer, no receptor; the chain bounces off the surface and drifts away, label "no receptor: nothing happens". Labels with leaders: "receptor", "cell membrane", "angiotensin II". Keep `data-part="receptor"` on the helices. Do not label it AT1 unless the caption is updated.

### Step 6 `squeeze` (grade 4)
Problems: arteriole is a red bar inside a tan bar with brown ellipses that do not thicken; angiotensin II hovers above the vessel and never lands; the dashed ghost sits on the outer wall at t=1 but on the lumen edge at t=0, so it is ambiguous what it marks; empty top third.
Redraw: arteriole in 3/4 cutaway: endothelium (thin pale layer), a sleeve of circumferential smooth-muscle cells (spindle-shaped, striated, `#c98a6a`), adventitia outline. The cutaway window shows the lumen with red blood and moving red cells (small biconcave discs) rather than chevrons. Angiotensin II chains land on the outer muscle surface. As t rises, each muscle spindle shortens and thickens, the outer diameter shrinks a little and the lumen shrinks a lot (lumen ghost dashed at its normal width, labelled). Blood cells slow and bunch upstream. A blue arrow pair pinches at the lumen with "narrower". Dial on the right (keep) with "rises" and a blue arrow. Labels: "arteriole", "smooth muscle", "lumen", "angiotensin II". `data-part="resistance"` should wrap the narrowed lumen plus its label.

### Step 7 `adrenal` (grade 2)
Problems: the adrenal gland is a tan triangle; the kidney is the bare bean again; angiotensin II never visibly docks; aldosterone hexagons arc through empty air; no blood vessel; no hint of where in the gland aldosterone is made.
Redraw: left: the kidney (same primitive as step 1, smaller, external view with capsule gradient) with an adrenal cap on the upper pole (right adrenal pyramid shape or left adrenal crescent; pick one and label "adrenal gland"), plus a cross-section inset (circle, upper right) of the adrenal showing a thin outer capsule, the outermost zona glomerulosa (thin band, highlighted), fasciculata, reticularis, and the medulla core; label only "outer layer makes aldosterone" (the caption does not name zones; adding the term "zona glomerulosa" is accurate but optional). Angiotensin II arrives in an adrenal artery, docks on the outer layer, and aldosterone (steroid glyph, see primitives) leaves via the adrenal vein into a drawn vessel that loops back to the kidney's renal artery. Keep `ALDO_PATH` as a rider but route it along the drawn vessel.

### Step 8 `salt` (grade 3)
Problems: the "tube cell" is a cream rounded box; sodium and potassium travel along straight arrows that pierce both membranes with no channels or pump, which is the core of the mechanism; the aldosterone receptor is a U-cup in mid-cytoplasm with no nucleus, although aldosterone acts on an intracellular receptor that switches on genes; "end of a nephron: becomes urine" floats under the lumen.
Redraw: a principal cell of the collecting duct (the caption says "near their end"; name it "tube cell" to match), drawn as a columnar epithelial cell with tight junctions to neighbour cells (partial neighbours top and bottom), a nucleus, apical membrane facing the tubular lumen (left, pale yellow urine wash) and basolateral membrane facing the interstitium and a capillary (right, red). Proteins: ENaC on the apical side (sodium channel, drawn as a barrel), ROMK on the apical side (potassium channel, different colour), and the Na+/K+ ATPase pump on the basolateral side (a larger protein with a rotating feel; 3 Na out, 2 K in). Aldosterone (steroid glyph) crosses the basolateral membrane, binds its receptor in the cytoplasm, the pair moves into the nucleus, and then more ENaC and pump units appear (fade in) on the membranes. Then sodium diamonds flow lumen -> ENaC -> cytoplasm -> pump -> blood; potassium circles flow blood -> pump -> cytoplasm -> ROMK -> lumen. Labels: "inside the tube (becomes urine)", "blood", "tube cell", "sodium channel", "potassium channel", "pump", "aldosterone". Keep `data-part="nephron"` on the lumen + its label. Accuracy: channel names may be shown only as plain words unless the caption gains ENaC/ROMK terms.

### Step 9 `volume` (grade 2)

## Risks
- Model-driven geometry: steps 2, 6, 9 and 10 to 15 read values from `chain()`/`steady()`; the needle angle (`needleAngle`), bar levels (`lvl`, NODE.level) and arteriole width must stay tied to the model, not hard-coded.
- `kidneyCloseUp` is shared by steps 1 and 2; `chainMap` is shared by steps 10 to 15 AND the Explore/Clinical tabs (two SVG copies need their own arrowhead ids, as the code comment notes).
- `data-part` names listed above must survive on the new structures, or dfn highlighting breaks (`block` appears four times in captions; every drug step must keep `data-part="block"` on its crosses).
- Ghost conventions (`class="ghost" data-ghost="normal"`) on dial, bars, arteriole and volume must remain; a "What changes it" step (12 to 15) must still morph from normal at t=0.
- Label overlap: the chain is dense; moving crosses onto arrows must not push them onto bars. Test at 375 x 553.
- Accuracy traps: JG cells are in the afferent arteriole wall, not at the hilum; ACE is on pulmonary (and other) endothelium, membrane-bound; angiotensin I is 10 residues, II is 8; aldosterone acts mainly on principal cells of the late distal tubule/collecting duct via an intracellular receptor that raises ENaC and Na+/K+ ATPase; K+ leaves via apical channels; the AT1 receptor is a GPCR; aldosterone comes from the zona glomerulosa. ARBs raise angiotensin II (keep). Do not draw bradykinin unless the ACE step gains a picture for it.
- File size: richer SVG per frame is re-rendered every animation tick (render(t) returns a string); heavy bilayers and gradients should be built once (defs, or `mount(host)` with only moving parts updated) to stay smooth on phones.
- style.css `.on-organ` halo colour must change if the organ fill colours change.
