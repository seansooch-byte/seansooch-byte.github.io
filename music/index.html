anabology's making-of, the recipe behind The GlassThe instructions

anabology published everything behind ESCAPE VELOCITY in a public Google Drive folder. It is what we read to make The Glass, and anyone can follow it. Five documents:

  1. README: how it was made.The stack (Midjourney for the stills, Suno for the song, Seedance 2.5 for the moving shots, a JavaScript engine for the type and graphics, Claude in Claude Code as the production team), the steps in order, and what worked.
  2. Orchestration: the prompts that ran the production.The original one prompt, how the work was split, and the research, lyrics, storyboard and animation workflows.
  3. Direction log.anabology's notes at every checkpoint, in order and nearly verbatim: how one director steered with a handful of decisive notes.
  4. Midjourney prompts.Every prompt, batch by batch, for the stills the film is built on.
  5. Seedance prompts.Every clip: the still as the image, the exact slice of the vocal as the audio, and how lip-sync was measured and cut just before it drifted.

What The Glass took: a singer generated from a still and the exact slice of vocal she sits under; sync measured, never eyeballed; meaning drawn in code on top of the footage; and the lead herself, credited. What it changed: Seedance would not take a photoreal face as its reference, so her shots run through a lip-sync model on closed-mouth stills, and our own meter checks the mouth against the voice.

Why this page starts with someone else's workWhy a bench

  1. They set the bar.ESCAPE VELOCITY is the strongest music video made with Claude that this site has seen, and every film after it on the bench comes from the same director with the same star. Everything under Music gets measured against them, not against nothing.
  2. She sings.In ESCAPE VELOCITY, Claudia lip-syncs the real vocal: every moving shot was generated from a still and the exact slice of the song it sits under. LAST LIGHT has no people on screen, so its pipeline never had to solve a singing character. The Glass is our first try.
  3. The method is public.ESCAPE VELOCITY's making-of is published in full: every prompt, the director's notes and the agent prompts. One director, about 50 messages, about 19 hours. A bench is only useful if you can see how the reference was made.
  4. One character, dated runs.The same idol, film after film, with the same spark in her hair. Like the editions on this site's home page, each new film is a dated run of the same test, so the change between runs is the thing to watch.

Every film on the bench is anabology's work, shown with credit and a link back. Streams are 720p.
