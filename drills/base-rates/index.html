Fictional cases. Educational, not for clinical use.

Base-rate risk trainer

Estimate how often a fictional alert is a true case. Then look at 1,000 fictional patients and see why that percent collapses when the condition is rare.

Why the base rate wins

Sensitivity is the share of true cases the alert catches. Specificity is the share of people without the condition whom the alert correctly leaves alone. PPV, positive predictive value, is the share of alerts that are true cases. NPV, negative predictive value, is the share of clear results that are truly clear.

At a low prevalence, almost everyone is free of the condition. A small false-alarm rate then creates more false alerts than true ones. PPV falls even when sensitivity and specificity stay the same. After you lock an estimate, move the prevalence slider and watch the same alert change jobs.

NNE, the number needed to evaluate, is how many alerts you read for each true case. It is 1 / PPV. It is the headline burden number, and it needs no treatment input. NNT, the number needed to treat, divides that burden by the fictional relative risk reduction of the action the alert triggers: NNT = 1 / (PPV x RRR) = NNE / RRR. NNT assumes the action helps only patients who would have had the event, at that fixed fictional RRR, and harms no one.

The formulas, with p for prevalence, Se for sensitivity and Sp for specificity, are:

  • PPV = Se x p / (Se x p + (1 - Sp) x (1 - p))
  • NPV = Sp x (1 - p) / (Sp x (1 - p) + (1 - Se) x p)
  • Risk after a positive alert = PPV. Risk after a clear result = 1 - NPV.
  • NNE = 1 / PPV. NNT = 1 / (PPV x RRR).

If specificity is 100% and the alert still flags someone, PPV is 100% because there are no false alerts. If the threshold flags nobody, PPV is undefined: the page says so, and it does not print 100%. If alerts exist but none are true cases, PPV is 0% and NNE and NNT are undefined. Percents are rounded to one decimal. A value that is not exactly 100% is not shown as 100%, and a value that is not exactly 0% is not shown as 0%.
